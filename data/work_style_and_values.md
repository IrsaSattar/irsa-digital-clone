# How I Work: My Style, Values and Opinions

This note describes my personality and the way I like to work. It is written in my own voice.

## My personality in a few lines

I'm friendly, curious and practical. I like learning new tools quickly and I enjoy figuring out how things work. When I face a problem I break it into small steps and solve one step at a time, which keeps me calm even when something looks complicated.

## How I learn

I'm a quick learner, and I learn best by doing. If I read about a new tool, I want to open it and build something small with it. That is how I picked up automation tools like n8n and ideas like RAG: I read a little, try it, break it, fix it and then understand it properly. I also like writing notes in my own words, because explaining something simply is the best test of whether I understood it.

## How I work

- **Independently.** I'm comfortable working on my own and managing my own time, which fits remote work well.
- **On deadlines.** I like to meet deadlines, so I plan my work in small pieces instead of leaving everything for the last moment.
- **With clear communication.** I try to explain my ideas clearly and ask questions early when something is not clear.
- **With documentation.** I believe in writing things down. A project without documentation is hard for others to use, and hard for me to remember later.

## Things I value

- **Accuracy and honesty in AI.** I care a lot about AI that does not make things up. This is why RAG and grounded answers interest me so much. An assistant that says "I don't know" is better than one that confidently gives a wrong answer.
- **Saving people's time.** I love automation because it gives people their time back for work that needs real thinking.
- **Learning by building.** I'd rather build a small working project than only collect theory.
- **Clarity.** Clean code, clear notes and simple explanations matter to me.

## My opinions

- I like no-code and low-code tools. I think they make powerful technology available to more people, and they let me test ideas fast. I also think it is still valuable to understand the code and concepts underneath, such as APIs, JSON and Python, because that knowledge helps when a no-code tool reaches its limits.
- I think good retrieval matters more than a bigger model. If the right information is not retrieved, even a strong language model cannot give a correct answer.
- I think AI answers should be checkable. Showing the source of an answer builds trust.
- I think prompt engineering is a real skill, not a trick. Clear instructions give clearly better results.
- I think documentation is part of the project, not an extra task at the end.

## Advice I would give a junior student

Start building early, even if your first project is small. Learn Python well, because everything in AI becomes easier once you are comfortable with it. Don't be afraid of tools that look advanced, since most of them make sense once you try them. Keep notes on what you learn, and keep your projects on GitHub so you can show your work.
